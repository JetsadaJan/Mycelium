# Biological Neural Network

A **Biological Neural [[Network]]** is an [[interconnected]] population of nerve cells (neurons) in the brain and nervous system linked through axons, dendrites, and chemical or electrical synapses. Strikingly, the topology and signaling dynamics of brain circuits mirror the underground [[Mycelium Network]] of [[Fungi]].

## Structural Parallels: Brain vs. Mycelium vs. AI

- **Soma & Nodes**: Each neuron cell body acts as a processing [[Node (Graph & Neural)|Node]] that integrates incoming signals before firing.
- **Axons & Hyphae**: Long axonal projections transmit action potentials to distant regions, functioning like biological [[Hypha (Biology)|Hyphae]] or software [[Hypha (Mycelium Engine)|Hyphae]].
- **Synaptic Plasticity**: Pathways that are repeatedly activated strengthen over time, inspiring the mathematical weights of an [[Artificial Neural Network]].

Comparative connectomics studies show that both the human cerebral cortex (containing approximately 86 billion neurons and 100 trillion synaptic connections) and forest mycelial networks optimize for "small-world" graph efficiency—minimizing wiring cost while maximizing fault-tolerant information transfer.
